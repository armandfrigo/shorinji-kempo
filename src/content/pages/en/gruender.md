## So Doshin — the man of the pine forests

**Born 11 February 1911, Okayama • Died May 1980**

### A childhood between cultures

At seventeen, So Doshin left Japan for China — then a rare step indeed. He sought wisdom. He found it with masters of the Shaolin temples, especially with Wen Laoshi at the renowned Songshan Shaolin academy.

Yet it was not only the **how** of the martial art that shaped him. **It was the *why* behind it** — Buddhist teaching that inner strength and outer technique are inseparable.

### The trauma that changed everything

1945: the Second World War ended. So Doshin was in Manchuria under Soviet occupation. He saw the worst: people destroying one another; whole societies coming apart.

**In that moment he knew: knowing techniques is not enough. You must cultivate people of genuine inner strength — people with compassion, courage, and a sense of justice.**

### The founding: October 1947

Back in Japan, in a town called Tadotsu (Kagawa), So Doshin opened the first Shorinji Kempo dōjō. It was not a place for competition or domination. **It was a place of transformation.**

His principle was simple and profound:

> **Ken Zen Ichinyo — body and mind as one**

You do not train only kicks and strikes. You train how you think, how you choose, how you meet fear. The body teaches the mind.

### The legacy

So Doshin died in 1980. Yet the system he created lives on. Today more than **three million people** practise Shorinji Kempo worldwide — not as rivals, but as learners.

His core idea is timeless:

> **Half for your own happiness, half for the happiness of others**
> (Half for one's own happiness, half for the happiness of others)

This is Shorinji Kempo.
