## Frequently asked questions

What you should know about Shorinji Kempo in Switzerland.

---

## **Why Shorinji Kempo?**

### Is Shorinji Kempo a competitive sport?

**No.** That is one of the defining differences. Shorinji Kempo is **not a combat sport** — there are no championships where you fight to beat someone else.

Instead you train **with** others, not against them. You help one another grow. That also makes practice safer and less injury-prone than many other martial arts.

### What will I actually learn?

You learn:
- **self-defence** — practical skills to stay safe
- **physical fitness** — strength, mobility, stamina
- **mental clarity** — focus, courage, inner calm
- **character** — how to meet difficulty with steadiness
- **community** — real friendships among practitioners

### Do I need prior experience?

**No.** Not at all. Shorinji Kempo suits **complete beginners**. Young or older, slight or strong — the system works for you.

Beginners train in the same room as black belts. Everyone works at their own level.

## **How do I begin?**

### What happens in the first class?

The first session is **free and without obligation**.

You simply show up, train, meet the instructors, and see whether it fits you. No contract, no pressure.

### What should I bring?

For your first visit you only need:
- **sport clothes** (sweatpants and T-shirt — nothing special)
- **socks** (we train barefoot or in socks)
- **water**

After a few weeks you will want a **gi** (the traditional Shorinji Kempo uniform), roughly CHF 80–150.

### How often should I train?

At the start, **once a week is enough**.

Later, many people train twice a week to progress faster.

There is no minimum attendance rule — come when you can.

### I am too old / too weak / overweight…

That **truly does not matter**.

Shorinji Kempo works *because* it rests on technique, not raw power. Older practitioners, different body types, people with limitations — everyone shares the same floor.

Your instructor will show you how to adapt techniques to your situation.

## **For parents: Shorinji Kempo for children**

### From what age?

We offer classes for:
- **children from about six years** (beginners)
- **teens from about twelve** (more advanced work)
- **adults** (always)

### What does it give my child?

Shorinji Kempo helps children:
- **build confidence** — stronger in body and mind
- **learn discipline** — respectful behaviour and focus
- **make friends** — training is shared with others
- **feel safer** — they study real self-defence

Unlike competitive fight sports, Shorinji Kempo tends to be **less injury-prone**, because the spirit is cooperative, not rivalrous.

### Can parents train too?

**Yes!** Many families practise together. Parents and children in the same dōjō is a memorable experience.

## **Belts & progress**

### How does the belt system work?

You begin as a **white belt** and move through coloured stages:
- white → yellow → orange → green → blue → brown → black

Each level has examinations, but they are **not contests against other people** — you are assessed on your own standard.

Most people need **about two or three years** to reach black belt.

### Must I test?

You may, but you do not have to. Some people train purely for enjoyment without chasing grades.

Examinations can be motivating — they give clear goals.

## **Culture & philosophy**

### What does “Zen” mean here?

**Zen** is a Buddhist tradition of meditation and insight. In Shorinji Kempo, active training meets quiet, focused mind.

You study more than kicks and strikes — you learn to stay **calm and centred** mentally.

That matters as much as the physical side.

### Must I be Buddhist?

**No.** No religious belief is required.

The teaching is ethical and practical — “half of your strength for your happiness, half for the happiness of others.” It speaks as well to atheists as to believers.

### Why “no competition”?

Because **real growth is not born from rivalry** — it grows through cooperation.

If you fight to win, you want to defeat the other person. In Shorinji Kempo you help your partner learn. The spirit is different.

Your “opponent” on the mat is your **teacher**, not your enemy.
