## Philosophy: more than techniques — a way of living

Shorinji Kempo rests on six foundational principles. They are not abstract theory — you will feel them in every class.

---

### 1. Ken Zen Ichinyo (拳禅一如)
## Body and mind are one

Your body and mind are not split. A fearful mind shows in hesitant movement. A clear mind supports precise technique.

In Shorinji Kempo you train **not only the body**. You train the mind as well: focus, courage, clarity.

**In life**: this balance helps you decide clearly and stay calm under pressure.

---

### 2. Riki Ai Funi (力愛不二)
## Strength and love cannot be divided

This is the heart of the art: **true strength without compassion is violence; compassion without strength cannot protect.**

You learn to be powerful — always with the aim to guard and to help, never to destroy.

**In life**: it means using your skills for what is right and carrying responsibility.

---

### 3. Shushu Koju (守柔敬重)
## Defence before attack

You are trained to protect yourself — first. Only once you are safe do you respond.

This is not cowardice. It is **wisdom**. A skilled practitioner avoids conflict whenever possible.

**In life**: meet tension with care. Understand first, then answer.

---

### 4. Fusatsu Katsujin (不殺活人)
## Protect; do not destroy

A fully developed technique lets your partner remain able to act. You need not break them — you need only stop the harm.

That is the highest standard: **maximum control, minimum injury.**

**In life**: even in conflict, stay respectful. Victory is not destroying others — growth comes through understanding.

---

### 5. Go Ju Ittai (剛柔一体)
## Hard and soft united

Every technique weaves both qualities: direct power and flowing adaptation — never only one pole.

That is real balance.

**In life**: some moments call for firmness, others for flexibility. The wisest choice often uses both.

---

### 6. Kumite Shutai (組手手相)
## Partnership, not rivalry

You do not train **against** your partner. You train **together**. Your partner helps you grow — and you help them.

This differs fundamentally from competitive sport. The point is not “winning.”

**In life**: the finest results come from sincere cooperation, not from constant contest.

---

## The aim of all six pillars

Together these principles sketch a life in balance:

- body and mind move as allies
- your strength serves others
- you act with care and good judgement
- you reach aims without breaking people
- you blend resolve with flexibility
- you grow through honest relationship

**This is not only the philosophy of a martial art. It is the philosophy of a life fully lived.**

---

## So Doshin said:

> **“Half for your happiness, half for the happiness of others.”**
> (Half for one's own happiness, half for the happiness of others)

That says it all.
